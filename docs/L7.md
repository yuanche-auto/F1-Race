## Part A – diagnosis of the nine L5 runs

*Mechanism first, method second.* The nine L5 runs (groups A, B, C × seeds 1–3) and the two
selected checkpoints that are not final (`B_s1_500k`, `C_s2_450k`) were re-driven on the 81
L5 test laps (51 L2 cars, 30 L3 disturbance seeds) with a log of every 50 ms decision
(`scripts/l7_partA.py`, worktree pinned at 1b99c0e; supplementary measures
`scripts/l7_partA_supp.py`; tables `results/L7/partA/partA.md`, `partA_supp.md`).

**Amendment A2.** The L5 test cars 0–24 (at τ = 0) and the 30 L3 seeds are the L7 test
sets T1 (τ = 0) and T2. Every finding is therefore given in two parts: **(i)** the L5 test
cars 25–50 (26 cars per checkpoint, outside the L7 test sets) and, for the blind spots over
training, the L5 validation outcomes; **(ii)** the laps of T1 (τ = 0) and T2 (55 per
checkpoint). Only (i) may change Part C; (ii) is reported for completeness and is not
used to support (i). Before A2 was committed, two laps of `B_s2` in (ii) had been seen in a
smoke test (disclosed in A2).

The intermediate checkpoints of the nine runs were not restored from the old machine, so
A2–A4 are on the final and the two selected checkpoints only; A1 over training uses the
logged validation outcomes. All numbers are descriptive (pooled decisions of few laps are
not independent); no test is attached to Part A.

### A0 Reproduction

All 891 laps (11 checkpoints × 81) end as in L5's `test.json`, every failure within 5 m of
the logged position (e.g. `B_s2` 33 of 81 completed, 48 of 48 failures at the same place).
The evaluation is deterministic across the two machines.

### A1 Blind spots and how they move

A blind spot of a checkpoint: a 100 m window that holds at least half of its failures, if it
has at least 3.

**(i) L5 test cars 25–50:** six of the nine checkpoints with failures have a blind spot –
`A_s3` 3 043–3 143 m (5 of 9 failures), `B_s1` 3 090 m (4 of 4), `B_s2` 4 130 m (12 of 17),
`C_s1` 3 781 m (11 of 11), `C_s2` 2 784 m (13 of 13), `C_s3` 3 070 m (11 of 13); `A_s1`
(7 failures), `A_s2` (1) and `C_s2_450k` (2) have none; `B_s3` and `B_s1_500k` do not fail.

**Over training (L5 validation outcomes, 20 laps per checkpoint):** up to 100–150k every run
fails at ~450 m (turn 1, all 20 laps or nearly); afterwards the blind spots move between a
few places – ~1 120–1 640 m (`A_s2` at 250k, `B_s1` at 100–150k, `A_s1` at 300k), ~2 730–2 850 m,
~3 010–3 090 m, ~3 760–3 850 m and ~4 130 m – and a run's blind spot can stay for several
checkpoints (`C_s2` at ~3 778 m from 250k to 400k; `C_s1` at ~3 760–3 790 m at 300–350k,
450k and 550–600k, at 3 090 m at 500k) or jump between them (`A_s1`: 2 730, 3 008, 2 840,
1 638, 2 788 … 4 129 m). Several runs share the same places (3 040–3 150 m:
`A_s3`, `B_s1`, `C_s3`; ~3 780 m: `C_s1`, `C_s2` in training, `A_s2`/`B_s2` early): **the
blind spots are corners of the track that are hard for these policies, and which of them a
given run (and checkpoint) fails at depends on the run** – a refinement of L5's "the blind
spot moves from run to run".

(ii) T1 (τ = 0) + T2: the same windows for `B_s2` (4 129 m, 30 of 31), `C_s1`, `C_s2`, `A_s3`;
`A_s1` and `A_s2` have a blind spot there (4 128 m, 2 839 m) and none in (i).

### A2 States before the failures (and A2b: which cars fail)

The last 3 s (60 decisions) before each failure against the same 100 m on the completed laps
of the same checkpoint and part (medians).

**(i):**

* **Speed relative to the plan: no difference.** v / v_plan before the failures equals the
  window on the completed laps (within 0.01) for seven of the nine checkpoints with failures
  (e.g. `A_s1` 0.86 / 0.86, `C_s1` 0.85 / 0.86, `C_s3` 0.93 / 0.93); lower in `B_s2`
  (0.90 / 0.93) and `C_s2` (0.88 / 0.94).
  The policies do not fail by going faster than on the laps that get through.
* **The tyres are worked harder.** Rear slip relative to the peak is higher before the
  failures in seven of nine (e.g. `B_s1` 0.61 / 0.45, `C_s3` 0.58 / 0.44, `A_s1` 0.47 / 0.39;
  not in `B_s2`, `C_s1`), the friction usage higher by ≥ 0.02 in six of nine (`A_s3` 0.91 / 0.84,
  `C_s3` 0.98 / 0.91, `C_s1` 0.87 / 0.81; equal in `B_s1`, `C_s2`, lower in `A_s2`).
* **Steering offset more extreme** where it differs: `B_s1` −0.88 / −0.35, `C_s3` −0.92 /
  −0.60, `A_s3` 0.20 / 0.04.
* **A2b – the failing cars have less grip.** Median friction factor of the failing cars
  against the completing ones: 0.937–0.972 against 0.984–1.007 in eight of the nine
  checkpoints with failures (range of the cars 0.92–1.03; `A_s2` with a single failure); the
  exception is `B_s2`, whose
  blind spot at 4 130 m catches cars of any friction (0.986 / 0.984). The yaw inertia shows
  no consistent difference.

So in (i) the typical failure is: a car with less grip, at the run's blind-spot corner, at the same
speed (relative to the plan) as the cars that get through, with the policy's steering
offset at an extreme and the rear tyres near or past their peak. The policy does not slow
down for the low-grip car: it behaves the same as for a car that gets through.

(ii): a similar pattern (speed ratio equal or lower; the failing cars with lower friction in
8 of the 9 checkpoints with failures, the exception `C_s2`).

### A3 Saturated actions

Fraction of decisions with an action component at ±0.99 or beyond: before the failures,
in the same window on completed laps, elsewhere.

**(i):** not a general mechanism – higher before failures than in the window for `A_s1`
(0.36 / 0.20), `C_s1` (0.76 / 0.48), `A_s2` (0.13 / 0.00, one failure), slightly for `B_s1`, `B_s2`,
`C_s2` (by 0.01–0.06); lower for `A_s3` (0.14 / 0.20), `C_s3` (0.27 / 0.39) and `C_s2_450k`
(0.77 / 0.95). The blind-spot windows are saturated more than the rest of the lap in `C_s1`
(0.48 against 0.21 elsewhere), `C_s3` (0.39 / 0.13) and `C_s2_450k` (0.95 / 0.20) – their
steering offset sits at its speed-dependent bound there – but not in `C_s2` (0.04 / 0.23).
The speed factor before failures is near its lower bound (0.85–0.88 in `A_s1`, `A_s2`, `A_s3`,
`C_s1`, `C_s2`, `C_s2_450k`) – and just as near on the completed laps at the same place, except in
`C_s2` (0.88 / 0.99).

(ii): the same mixture.

### A4 The critic near the failures

Q = min(Q₁, Q₂)(s, a) of the checkpoint's critic, G the realised discounted return
(γ = 0.99, the training reward including the −50 crash penalty; decisions in the last 300 of
a completed lap excluded because G is truncated at the lap end).

**(i):**

* **Q − G**: +34.5 to +57.6 in the last 3 s before the failures (eight of the nine
  checkpoints), against −13.3 to −17.4 elsewhere; for `C_s2_450k` −3.1 against −22.2. The
  critic overestimates the return before a failure by 50–75 relative to elsewhere (19 for
  `C_s2_450k`) – about the discounted crash penalty: **it does not anticipate the crash.**
* **A4b – does it tell the failing state from a passing one at the same place?** Mostly not:
  the median Q before the failures is only 1.2–5.4 below Q at the same 100 m on the completed
  laps for six of the nine checkpoints (`B_s2` −1.2 with 17 failures, `A_s3` −1.7, `C_s2` −1.5);
  clearly lower for `B_s1` (−8.9), `C_s3` (−17.4) and `C_s2_450k` (−31.3). For comparison, the crash
  penalty discounted over the 3 s is −27 to −50.

(ii): Q − G +17.0 to +61.5 before failures against −13.1 to −17.6 elsewhere; Q before the
failures 1.0–5.3 below the window for most checkpoints, −11.7 (`B_s1`) and −26.5 (`C_s3`).

### What Part A says about the mechanism – and about Part C

From (i) alone:

1. The blind spots are a handful of hard corners; which one a run fails at depends on the
   run and moves during training (A1).
2. At those corners the failing laps are mostly cars with less grip, driven at the same
   relative speed as the cars that get through, with the tyres worked harder (A2, A2b).
3. Neither the actions (A3) nor the critic (A4, A4b) distinguish the failing car from a
   passing one at the same place in most runs: the critic's value before the failure is
   almost that of a passing lap, and the crash is not anticipated. The history of eight
   observations, which was meant to let the policy infer the car, does not lead it to treat
   the low-grip car differently at these corners.

This is consistent with the blind spot being a **value error at a few states that the
training distribution makes rare** (a low-grip car at a particular corner): the critic has
not learned that these states end in a crash, so the policy has no reason to avoid them.
It does not separate whether the cause is too little experience of these states or an
observation that cannot reveal the car's grip in time; the protocol's Part C addresses the
first (episodes started before failure positions, F and M) and not the second.

**Consequence for Part C: no change.** The mechanism found in (i) is what the failure-start
curriculum of F and M is designed for; nothing in (i) asks for a different design, and
nothing from (ii) is used. Two observations are recorded for the report but do not change
Part C: the speed factor is often near its lower bound (0.85) at the blind spots, also on
laps that get through, and the steering offset of `C_s1`, `C_s3` and `C_s2_450k` sits at its
bound there.
