Notes fixed before the results were read:

* **Equivalence of the acados OCP** (section 4; validation set V, 200 problems from 40 IPOPT laps):
  95.5 % within 2 % against the criterion of 95 % – met, by 0.5 points. Per configuration: `nmpc` 98 %,
  `nmpc_reserve` **92 %**, `smpc` 98 %, `lag` **94 %**. Of the 9 problems outside 2 %, in 4 acados did

* **Why A1 and A2 exist** (protocol amendments): in the smoke laps on X, under the rule of revision 1,
  every IPOPT controller left the track within 70–120 m. Under A1 they still left it within 94–180 m.
  A step-by-step trace (`scripts/l9_debug_rt.py`; nominal car, `nmpc_reserve`, the start straight)
  showed the mechanism. Each solve takes ~85–95 ms, so segments 0–1 of every plan are never executed.
  With the steering *rate* as input, the real steering angle drifts from the plan's, and the commanded
  rate roughly doubled every 0.1 s (−0.009 rad/s at 0.6 s, −0.117 at 1.0 s, −0.47 at 1.2 s) until the
  car left the track at 129 m. A2 adds an arm with delay compensation, L̂ = 100 ms, **fixed in advance
  and not tuned**.

## Primary tests: H-RT1 and H-RT2 hold

`lag_identified` (exploration lap, identification, re-plan, lag-aware NMPC), the 105 laps of T1new + T2new,
exact McNemar, Holm (and each at 0.025):

| test | comparison | completed | only first : only second | p | Holm | at 0.025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **H-RT1** | IPOPT offline vs IPOPT real-time | **88** vs **0** | 88 : 0 | 6.5·10⁻²⁷ | holds (level 0.025) | holds |
| **H-RT2** | real-time: RTI vs IPOPT | **79** vs **0** | 79 : 0 | 3.3·10⁻²⁴ | holds (level 0.05) | holds |

Under the real-time rule (one vCPU per controller, 50 ms period, a command takes effect when its
computation has finished, rounded up to the 10 ms step), the IPOPT implementation completes none of the
laps it completes offline. Every solve missed the 50 ms deadline (miss rate 1.000), with a mean latency
of 232 ms (P95 366 ms). Every lap ended at the start (distance P10 / P50 / P90 72 / 126 / 177 m;
lap 4 315 m). The acados RTI implementation of the same optimal control problem completes 79 of 105
under the same rule, with a feedback latency of 2.4 ms mean (P99 11 ms, no deadline miss).

H-RT1 measures the controller as deployed: its exploration lap also runs in real time. The real-time
IPOPT exploration laps also ended at the start (P50 104 m), so the identification rests on the first ~100–170 m
of driving. The secondary comparison (l9-m2) separates the two effects: with the identification and
re-plan from the **offline** exploration lap, the real-time IPOPT lap still completes 0 / 105. The
evaluated lap alone is enough to lose every lap. For RTI the offline exploration changes little: 75
against 79 (3 : 7, p = 0.34).

Scope: this compares two implementations of one controller on this machine and under this rule. IPOPT
here is the implementation of L6–L8 (CasADi and IPOPT; mean 147–344 ms per solve here). A
faster IPOPT setup, or a longer control period, was not tested.

* **Every IPOPT controller completes 0 / 105 in real time**, and every one of those laps ends within the
  first ~180 m (P90 100–177 m). Mean latency 147–344 ms, every deadline missed. Each offline-vs-real-time
  loss is significant (p 4.8·10⁻⁷ to 6.5·10⁻²⁷, uncorrected).
* **RTI loses nothing to the budget.** Offline against real-time: `nmpc` 3 : 0 (p 0.25), `nmpc_reserve`
  5 : 2 (0.45), `smpc` **1 : 11 (0.006)**, `identified` 2 : 2 (1), `lag_identified` 3 : 8 (0.23).
  Lap times on laps both complete differ by −0.07 to +0.20 s on average (exact signed-rank, supplement:
  `nmpc` +0.12 s, p 0.014; `identified` −0.07 s, p 0.007; `smpc` −0.03 s, p 0.023; the others
  p ≥ 0.55; 5 comparisons, uncorrected). That `smpc` RTI completes *more* laps in real time than
  offline is not explained. The real-time mode differs from the offline one in two ways: the untimed
  pre-start plan converged with 30 iterations (A1.4), and the 10 ms latency, during which the previous
  plan continues. But the two modes' laps end at the same places (supplement), so the pre-start plan
  does not show as fewer early failures. Not examined further. The repeat (below) puts the
  run-to-run noise of RTI at about 1 lap in 20.
* **What one RTI step costs without a time limit**, offline RTI against offline IPOPT: fewer laps for
  every controller – `lag_identified` 0 : 14 (p 1.2·10⁻⁴), `smpc` 1 : 17, `nmpc_reserve` 3 : 18, `nmpc`
  1 : 10, `identified` 1 : 10 (p 1.2·10⁻⁴ to 0.012). Real-time RTI against offline IPOPT (the question
  of section 1; supplement, post hoc): `lag_identified` 79 vs 88 (2 : 11, p 0.023), `smpc` 68 vs 74
  (3 : 9, p 0.15), `identified` 28 vs 37 (2 : 11, p 0.023), `nmpc_reserve` 14 vs 32, `nmpc` 10 vs 22
  (p < 0.001). The real-time formulation keeps most of what the offline NMPC achieved, but not all.
  What it loses comes from the approximation (one Gauss–Newton step per period), not from the budget.
* **Ranking** (section 7; more laps first): offline, the IPOPT and RTI rows interleave with
  `lag_identified` / IPOPT first. In real time every RTI row comes before every IPOPT row. Kendall's τ
  between the modes is 0.00 (descriptive). Within RTI the order of the five controllers is the same
  in both modes, and the same as IPOPT's offline: `lag_identified` > `smpc` > `identified` >
  `nmpc_reserve` > `nmpc`. Real-time IPOPT completes nothing. The budget does not reorder the controllers of L2–L8; it decides which implementation can
  run at all.
* **Fallbacks / failed QPs** per controller and mode are in the computation table of the report (RTI
  real-time 0–7 per 105 laps).

A 20 ms sensor delay, plus the 10 ms command latency, removes every lap of the RTI NMPC. Its laps

The RL policy loses 10 laps. The NMPC has no delay compensation. The exploration laps of this condition

undelayed identification.

## Reading

* **H-RT1 and H-RT2 hold.** The offline NMPC of L6–L8 (IPOPT) loses every lap under a one-core,
  50 ms budget, and an acados RTI formulation of the same problem keeps most of them: 79 laps against the offline
  IPOPT's 88 (2 : 11).
  The cost sits in the evaluated lap itself (l9-m2), and a fixed 100 ms delay compensation does not
  remove it (A2).
* **The budget changes which implementation can run, not which controller is better.** The order of
  the five model-based controllers is the same offline (both implementations) and in real time
  (RTI). RTI pays for
  its single iteration (fewer laps than converged IPOPT offline) but nothing more for running in real
  time.
* **RL is nearly insensitive to the budget** (sub-millisecond inference). Under it the RL policies
  complete more laps than the best real-time NMPC on these laps.
* **Delay is the NMPC's weak point.** A 20 ms sensor delay, uncompensated, takes the RTI NMPC from
  79 to 0. The same lack of compensation is behind the IPOPT failure mechanism traced on X.
  **Interpretation, not tested in L9:** a delay-compensated RTI (predicting the state over the known
  latency, as A2 does for IPOPT) is the obvious next step.

**Not verified / limits:**

* The second environment was not run (section 10; L7 A10, L8 section 9): H-RT1 and H-RT2 are not
  cross-checked.
* The equivalence of the acados OCP met its criterion by 0.5 points, and two configurations are below
  95 % on their own.
* One machine, one OS. Real-time outcomes depend on measured times: the repeat shows ~1 flip in 20
  for RTI.
* L̂ was fixed, not tuned. No faster IPOPT setup and no longer control period were tested.
* The exploration of the delay condition was undelayed.
* That `smpc` RTI completes more laps in real time than offline is unexplained.
* The one crashed job was re-run, and its cause is undetermined.
* The supplement (signed-rank lap times, real-time RTI vs offline IPOPT, distances, load per run) was
  written after the report had been read. report_l9.py at a881aee does not compute the signed-rank test
  of section 8.
