## Part A – diagnosis of the nine L5 runs

*Mechanism first, method second.* The nine L5 runs (groups A, B, C × seeds 1–3) and the two
selected checkpoints that are not final (`B_s1_500k`, `C_s2_450k`) were re-driven on the 81
L5 test laps (51 L2 cars, 30 L3 disturbance seeds) with a log of every 50 ms decision
(`scripts/l7_partA.py`, worktree pinned at 1b99c0e; supplementary measures
`scripts/l7_partA_supp.py`; tables `results/L7/partA/partA.md`, `partA_supp.md`).

**Amendment A2.** The L5 test cars 0–24 (at τ = 0) and the 30 L3 seeds are the L7 test
sets T1 (τ = 0) and T2. Every finding is therefore given in two parts: **(i)** the L5 test
cars 25–50 (26 cars per checkpoint, outside the L7 test sets) and, for the blind spots over
training, the L5 validation outcomes; **(ii)** the laps of T1 (τ = 0) and T2 (55 per
checkpoint). Only (i) may change Part C; (ii) is reported for completeness and is not
used to support (i). Before A2 was committed, two laps of `B_s2` in (ii) had been seen in a
smoke test (disclosed in A2).

The intermediate checkpoints of the nine runs were not restored from the old machine, so
A2–A4 are on the final and the two selected checkpoints only; A1 over training uses the
logged validation outcomes. All numbers are descriptive (pooled decisions of few laps are
not independent); no test is attached to Part A. Statistics: medians over decisions where a
typical state is described (A2, A3, A4, A4b), means where calibration is asked (A4c: a
calibrated critic matches the *expected* return).

**Revision after the review of 735c348 (CHANGES_REQUESTED):** A4's "the critic does not
anticipate the crash" was a selection effect (PA-M1) – replaced by A4c, a measure that does

### A0 Reproduction

All 891 laps (11 checkpoints × 81) end as in L5's `test.json`, every failure within 5 m of
the logged position (e.g. `B_s2` 33 of 81 completed, 48 of 48 failures at the same place).
The evaluation is deterministic across the two machines.

### A1 Blind spots and how they move

A blind spot of a checkpoint: a 100 m window that holds at least half of its failures, if it
has at least 3.

**(i) L5 test cars 25–50:** six of the nine checkpoints with failures have a blind spot –
`A_s3` 3 043–3 143 m (5 of 9 failures), `B_s1` 3 090 m (4 of 4), `B_s2` 4 130 m (12 of 17),
`C_s1` 3 781 m (11 of 11), `C_s2` 2 784 m (13 of 13), `C_s3` 3 070 m (11 of 13); `A_s1`
(7 failures), `A_s2` (1) and `C_s2_450k` (2) have none; `B_s3` and `B_s1_500k` do not fail.

**Over training (L5 validation outcomes, 20 laps per checkpoint):** up to 100–150k every run
fails at ~450 m (turn 1, all 20 laps or nearly); afterwards the blind spots move between a
few places – ~1 120–1 640 m (`A_s2` at 250k, `B_s1` at 100–150k, `A_s1` at 300k), ~2 730–2 850 m,
~3 010–3 090 m, ~3 760–3 850 m and ~4 130 m – and a run's blind spot can stay for several
checkpoints (`C_s2` at ~3 778 m from 250k to 400k; `C_s1` at ~3 760–3 790 m at 300–350k,
450k and 550–600k, at 3 090 m at 500k) or jump between them (`A_s1`: 2 730, 3 008, 2 840,
1 638, 2 788 … 4 129 m). Several runs share the same places (3 040–3 150 m:
`A_s3`, `B_s1`, `C_s3`; ~3 780 m: `C_s1`, `C_s2` in training, `A_s2`/`B_s2` early): **the
blind spots are corners of the track that are hard for these policies, and which of them a
given run (and checkpoint) fails at depends on the run** – a refinement of L5's "the blind
spot moves from run to run".

(ii) T1 (τ = 0) + T2: the same windows for `B_s2` (4 129 m, 30 of 31), `C_s1`, `C_s2`, `A_s3`;
`A_s1` and `A_s2` have a blind spot there (4 128 m, 2 839 m) and none in (i).

### A2 States before the failures (and A2b: which cars fail)

The last 3 s (60 decisions) before each failure against the same 100 m on the completed laps
of the same checkpoint and part (medians).

**(i):**

* **Speed relative to the plan: no difference.** v / v_plan before the failures equals the
  window on the completed laps (within 0.01) for seven of the nine checkpoints with failures
  (e.g. `A_s1` 0.86 / 0.86, `C_s1` 0.85 / 0.86, `C_s3` 0.93 / 0.93); lower in `B_s2`
  (0.90 / 0.93) and `C_s2` (0.88 / 0.94).
  The policies do not fail by going faster than on the laps that get through.
* **The tyres are worked harder.** Rear slip relative to the peak is higher before the
  failures in seven of nine (e.g. `B_s1` 0.61 / 0.45, `C_s3` 0.58 / 0.44, `A_s1` 0.47 / 0.39;
  not in `B_s2`, `C_s1`), the friction usage higher by ≥ 0.02 in six of nine (`A_s3` 0.91 / 0.84,
  `C_s3` 0.98 / 0.91, `C_s1` 0.87 / 0.81; equal in `B_s1`, `C_s2`, lower in `A_s2`).
* **Steering offset more extreme** where it differs: `B_s1` −0.88 / −0.35, `C_s3` −0.92 /
  −0.60, `A_s3` 0.20 / 0.04.
* **A2b – the failing cars have less grip.** Median friction factor of the failing cars
  against the completing ones: 0.937–0.972 against 0.984–1.007 in eight of the nine
  checkpoints with failures (range of the cars 0.92–1.03; `A_s2` with a single failure); the
  exception is `B_s2`, whose
  blind spot at 4 130 m catches cars of any friction (0.986 / 0.984). The yaw inertia shows
  no consistent difference.

So in (i) the typical failure is: a car with less grip, at the run's blind-spot corner, at the same
speed (relative to the plan) as the cars that get through, with the policy's steering
offset at an extreme and the rear tyres near or past their peak. The policy does not slow
down for the low-grip car: it behaves the same as for a car that gets through.

(ii): a similar pattern (speed ratio equal or lower; the failing cars with lower friction in
8 of the 9 checkpoints with failures, the exception `C_s2`).

### A3 Saturated actions

Fraction of decisions with an action component at ±0.99 or beyond: before the failures,
in the same window on completed laps, elsewhere.

**(i):** not a general mechanism – higher before failures than in the window for `A_s1`
(0.36 / 0.20), `C_s1` (0.76 / 0.48), `A_s2` (0.13 / 0.00, one failure), slightly for `B_s1`, `B_s2`,
`C_s2` (by 0.01–0.06); lower for `A_s3` (0.14 / 0.20), `C_s3` (0.27 / 0.39) and `C_s2_450k`
(0.77 / 0.95). The blind-spot windows are saturated more than the rest of the lap in `C_s1`
(0.48 against 0.21 elsewhere), `C_s3` (0.39 / 0.13) and `C_s2_450k` (0.95 / 0.20) – their
steering offset sits at its speed-dependent bound there – but not in `C_s2` (0.04 / 0.23).
The speed factor before failures is near its lower bound (0.85–0.88 in `A_s1`, `A_s2`, `A_s3`,
`C_s1`, `C_s2`, `C_s2_450k`) – and just as near on the completed laps at the same place, except in
`C_s2` (0.88 / 0.99).

(ii): the same mixture.

### A4 The critic near the failures

Q = min(Q₁, Q₂)(s, a) of the checkpoint's critic, G the realised discounted return
(γ = 0.99, the training reward including the −50 crash penalty).

**(i):**

* **A4 as pre-registered – not evidence about anticipation.** Q − G (median) is +34.5 to
  +57.6 in the last 3 s before the failures (eight of the nine checkpoints with failures),
  −13.3 to −17.4 elsewhere (decisions in the last 300 of a completed lap excluded, G being
  truncated at the line); `C_s2_450k` −3.1 / −22.2. Selecting the failed laps makes Q − G
  positive even for a perfectly calibrated critic: their G contains the crash penalty and
  lacks everything after the crash. These numbers therefore say nothing about whether the
  critic anticipates the crash (review PA-M1); they are kept only because A4 was
  pre-registered.
* **A4c – calibration at the blind spots, all laps** (supplementary, added after the review;
  `l7_partA_supp.py`). On the 100 m before each part-(i) failure position, all part-(i) laps
  of the checkpoint – failed and completed – are pooled, and the *mean* Q − G there is
  compared with the mean elsewhere on the same laps (the critic's overall offset). For this
  the 26 part-(i) laps were re-driven with each completed lap driven on for 300 decisions
  past the line, so that no G is truncated (`l7_partA.py drive --extend 300`, worktree at
  0ea964a; the first laps are identical to the original ones, checked decision by decision
  on three laps; completions as before). Excess = window − elsewhere; 0 for a calibrated
  critic:

  | checkpoint | failures (i) | window decisions (on failed laps) | excess | Q − G in the window: completed / failed laps |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | B_s1 | 4 | 1 660 (191) | +0.1 | −19.1 / +34.0 |
  | A_s2 | 1 | 1 329 (45) | +0.1 | −20.0 / +58.7 |
  | A_s1 | 7 | 4 986 (815) | +0.3 | −18.2 / +13.8 |
  | C_s3 | 13 | 2 352 (812) | +3.4 | −30.2 / +21.9 |
  | A_s3 | 9 | 2 333 (572) | +6.3 | −21.1 / +40.5 |
  | C_s1 | 11 | 1 618 (487) | +7.7 | −22.1 / +27.2 |
  | B_s2 | 17 | 3 042 (1 824) | +11.5 | −21.8 / +9.3 |
  | C_s2 | 13 | 1 488 (648) | +19.5 | −21.2 / +34.2 |
  | C_s2_450k | 2 | 1 250 (75) | −10.3 | −37.4 / +8.2 |

  On average over the cars, the critic is **calibrated at the blind spots of three
  checkpoints** (`B_s1`, `A_s2`, `A_s1`: within 0.3), **overestimates the return there in
  five** – moderately in `C_s3`, `A_s3`, `C_s1` (+3 to +8), clearly in `B_s2` (+11.5) and `C_s2`
  (+19.5; all its 13 part-(i) failures fall in one window) – and underestimates it in
  `C_s2_450k` (−10.3). The rough estimate in the review (truncated G) had `B_s2` at +28.8:
  driving on past the line matters for a blind spot 185 m before it.
* **A4b – does the critic tell the failing car from a passing one at the same place?**
  (median Q, supplementary): in six of the nine checkpoints hardly – Q in the last 3 s before
  the failures is 1.2–5.4 below Q on the same 100 m of the completed laps (`B_s2` −1.2 with
  17 failures, `A_s3` −1.7, `C_s2` −1.5) – while the return there differs by about 30–80 between
  failed and completed laps (A4c table). In `B_s1`, `C_s3` and `C_s2_450k` Q is clearly lower
  before the failures (−8.9, −17.4, −31.3).

(ii): A4 +17.0 to +61.5 before the failures against −13.1 to −17.6 elsewhere (the same
selection effect); A4b 1.0–5.3 below the window for most checkpoints, −11.7 (`B_s1`) and
−26.5 (`C_s3`). A4c was computed for (i) only.

### What Part A says about the mechanism – and about Part C

From (i) alone:

1. The blind spots are a handful of hard corners; which one a run fails at depends on the
   run and moves during training (A1).
2. At those corners the failing laps are mostly cars with less grip (μ 0.937–0.972 against
   0.984–1.007), driven at the same speed relative to the plan as the cars that get
   through, with the tyres worked harder (A2, A2b).
3. In six of the nine checkpoints with failures, neither the actions (A3) nor the critic's
   value (A4b) differ much between the failing car and a passing one at the same place; in
   `B_s1`, `C_s3` and `C_s2_450k` the critic's value is clearly lower before the failures.
4. Averaged over the cars, the critic overestimates the return at the blind spots in five
   checkpoints (most in `C_s2` and `B_s2`), is calibrated in three, underestimates in one
   (A4c).

Two explanations fit these findings, and Part A cannot tell them apart:

* **(a) A value error from too little experience** of the states that end in a crash: the
  critic has not learned that this car at this corner, driven this way, crashes – the
  overestimation of A4c at the blind spots of `C_s2`, `B_s2`, `C_s1`, `A_s3`. But the cars that
  fail are not rare in training: the training cars have μ ~ U(0.92, 1.03) (the L2 ranges,
  `experiments/l2.PERTURBATION`), so about a third of them have μ < 0.96, and episodes start
  at random points of the lap – low-grip cars pass these corners often in training. What
  may be rare is the particular state the run gets wrong, not the car.
* **(b) Observability**: the policy sees the last eight observations (0.4 s); if they do not
  reveal the car's grip before it reaches the corner, the policy can only drive one way for
  all cars, and that way is too aggressive for the low-grip ones. This fits the same
  relative speed and actions on failing and passing laps (A2, A3), the near-equal values
  (A4b), and a critic that is calibrated *over the cars* but cannot separate them – A4c ≈ 0
  in `B_s1`, `A_s1`, `A_s2`.

A test that could separate them – whether the observations before a blind-spot corner
predict the car's friction – was not made (optional in the review).

**Consequence for Part C: no change.** F and M add crash experience at the failure positions
(and, in M, the filter's interventions); under (a) that supplies the missing experience,
under (b) it can still teach the policy to be more cautious at these corners for every car
(at a cost in lap time) – Part A cannot say which, or whether either works; that is what S1
tests. Nothing from (ii) is used. Two observations are recorded for the report but do not
change Part C: the speed factor is often near its lower bound (0.85) at the blind spots,
also on laps that get through, and the steering offset of `C_s1`, `C_s3` and `C_s2_450k` sits
at its bound there.
