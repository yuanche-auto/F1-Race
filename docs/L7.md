selected checkpoints that are not final (`B_s1_500k`, `C_s2_450k`) were re-driven on the 81

### A1 Blind spots and how they move

A blind spot of a checkpoint: a 100 m window that holds at least half of its failures, if it
has at least 3.

**(i) L5 test cars 25–50:** six of the nine checkpoints with failures have a blind spot –
`A_s3` 3 043–3 143 m (5 of 9 failures), `B_s1` 3 090 m (4 of 4), `B_s2` 4 130 m (12 of 17),
`C_s1` 3 781 m (11 of 11), `C_s2` 2 784 m (13 of 13), `C_s3` 3 070 m (11 of 13); `A_s1`
(7 failures), `A_s2` (1) and `C_s2_450k` (2) have none; `B_s3` and `B_s1_500k` do not fail.

**Over training (L5 validation outcomes, 20 laps per checkpoint):** up to 100–150k every run
fails at ~450 m (turn 1, all 20 laps or nearly); afterwards the blind spots move between a
few places – ~1 120–1 640 m (`A_s2` at 250k, `B_s1` at 100–150k, `A_s1` at 300k), ~2 730–2 850 m,
~3 010–3 090 m, ~3 760–3 850 m and ~4 130 m – and a run's blind spot can stay for several
checkpoints (`C_s2` at ~3 778 m from 250k to 400k; `C_s1` at ~3 760–3 790 m at 300–350k,
450k and 550–600k, at 3 090 m at 500k) or jump between them (`A_s1`: 2 730, 3 008, 2 840,
1 638, 2 788 … 4 129 m). Several runs share the same places (3 040–3 150 m:
`A_s3`, `B_s1`, `C_s3`; ~3 780 m: `C_s1`, `C_s2` in training, `A_s2`/`B_s2` early): **the
blind spots are corners of the track that are hard for these policies, and which of them a
given run (and checkpoint) fails at depends on the run** – a refinement of L5's "the blind
spot moves from run to run".

(ii) T1 (τ = 0) + T2: the same windows for `B_s2` (4 129 m, 30 of 31), `C_s1`, `C_s2`, `A_s3`;
`A_s1` and `A_s2` have a blind spot there (4 128 m, 2 839 m) and none in (i).

### A2 States before the failures (and A2b: which cars fail)

of the same checkpoint and part (medians).

**(i):**

So in (i) the typical failure is: a car with less grip, at the run's blind-spot corner, at the same

(ii): a similar pattern (speed ratio equal or lower; the failing cars with lower friction in

### A4 The critic near the failures

Q = min(Q₁, Q₂)(s, a) of the checkpoint's critic, G the realised discounted return
(γ = 0.99, the training reward including the −50 crash penalty).

**(i):**

* **A4 as pre-registered – not evidence about anticipation.** Q − G (median) is +34.5 to
  +57.6 in the last 3 s before the failures (eight of the nine checkpoints with failures),
  −13.3 to −17.4 elsewhere (decisions in the last 300 of a completed lap excluded, G being
  truncated at the line); `C_s2_450k` −3.1 / −22.2. Selecting the failed laps makes Q − G
  positive even for a perfectly calibrated critic: their G contains the crash penalty and
  lacks everything after the crash. These numbers therefore say nothing about whether the
  critic anticipates the crash (review PA-M1); they are kept only because A4 was

### What Part A says about the mechanism – and about Part C

From (i) alone:

1. The blind spots are a handful of hard corners; which one a run fails at depends on the
   run and moves during training (A1).
2. At those corners the failing laps are mostly cars with less grip (μ 0.937–0.972 against
   0.984–1.007), driven at the same speed relative to the plan as the cars that get
   through, with the tyres worked harder (A2, A2b).
3. In six of the nine checkpoints with failures, neither the actions (A3) nor the critic's
   value (A4b) differ much between the failing car and a passing one at the same place; in
   `B_s1`, `C_s3` and `C_s2_450k` the critic's value is clearly lower before the failures.
4. Averaged over the cars, the critic overestimates the return at the blind spots in five
   checkpoints (most in `C_s2` and `B_s2`), is calibrated in three, underestimates in one
   (A4c).

Two explanations fit these findings, and Part A cannot tell them apart:

* **(a) A value error from too little experience** of the states that end in a crash: the
  critic has not learned that this car at this corner, driven this way, crashes – the
  overestimation of A4c at the blind spots of `C_s2`, `B_s2`, `C_s1`, `A_s3`. But the cars that
  fail are not rare in training: the training cars have μ ~ U(0.92, 1.03) (the L2 ranges,
  `experiments/l2.PERTURBATION`), so about a third of them have μ < 0.96, and episodes start
  at random points of the lap – low-grip cars pass these corners often in training. What
  may be rare is the particular state the run gets wrong, not the car.
* **(b) Observability**: the policy sees the last eight observations (0.4 s); if they do not
  reveal the car's grip before it reaches the corner, the policy can only drive one way for
  all cars, and that way is too aggressive for the low-grip ones. This fits the same
  relative speed and actions on failing and passing laps (A2, A3), the near-equal values
  (A4b), and a critic that is calibrated *over the cars* but cannot separate them – A4c ≈ 0
  in `B_s1`, `A_s1`, `A_s2`.

A test that could separate them – whether the observations before a blind-spot corner
predict the car's friction – was not made (optional in the review).

**Consequence for Part C: no change.** F and M add crash experience at the failure positions
(and, in M, the filter's interventions); under (a) that supplies the missing experience,
under (b) it can still teach the policy to be more cautious at these corners for every car
(at a cost in lap time) – Part A cannot say which, or whether either works; that is what S1
tests. Nothing from (ii) is used. Two observations are recorded for the report but do not
change Part C: the speed factor is often near its lower bound (0.85) at the blind spots,
also on laps that get through, and the steering offset of `C_s1`, `C_s3` and `C_s2_450k` sits
at its bound there.

`... l7_partA.py drive --all --extend 300` (A4c, ~1 min), `... l7_partA.py analyze`,
`... l7_partA_supp.py`.

  these changes was needed. Limitation (pb2-m3): the definition looks only at leaving the track
  or spinning within 3 s. An intervention whose effect shows later (for example on the braking
  point of the next corner) is counted as false, so the rates are an upper bound on the share of
  unneeded interventions.
* **Failures with the main filter** (pb-m2, last 3 s): of 667, **383 after a run of at least
  three solver fallbacks** (the NLP with the calibrated back-offs failing repeatedly, the car
  following the shifted plan), 284 after interventions, none after neither; 421 of the 667 laps
  complete without the filter. The categories are ordered (a fallback run counts before an
  intervention) and can occur together (pb2-m5): all 383 laps counted as fallback runs also
  have changed decisions in their last 3 s (a fallback command counts as changed), and in 330
  of them a decision whose solve succeeded changed the command, i.e. an intervention in the
  narrow sense; 53 have no such intervention.
* **The CBF-QP** completes no lap of any policy (all at turn 1), as on K (A6).
* **The bare tracker** gains from the main filter (6 → 14, 13 : 5, p = 0.096, not significant):
  the only policy that the filter helps is the one that fails most without it.

### Reading

* With a risk estimate that passes the acceptance test (its band covers 93 % of the model's
  errors at k = 10 and 20), this NMPC safety filter does not make the learned policies more
  reliable – it makes them less reliable, for every one of the nine runs and the selected
  policy. Two mechanisms, both measured: (i) **numerical** – the NLP with the calibrated
  back-offs fails in 0.5–7 % of the decisions, and runs of failures with the shifted-plan
  fallback precede 57 % of the filtered failures; (ii) **unnecessary interventions** – about a
  third of the decisions are changed, and 88–99 % of the sampled changes are not needed by the
  3 s criterion (the unfiltered policy gets through), at +1.5–3.3 s per lap.
* **Scope (pb2-m1).** The result holds for the filter evaluated here: N = 30, the nominal model
  with the back-offs of estimate (b), and the L6 failure handling (no retry, fallback to the
  shifted plan). It is not a result about safety filters in general. In particular a filter
  with retries was not evaluated in L7: in the preparation (`docs/prep_robustness.md`) the
  retries removed almost all failed solves of the NMPC (1 063 fallbacks → 5 in 18 paired
  trials) but did not complete more laps in that NMPC tracking setting (6 against 9 of 18,
  3 : 0, p = 0.25), which is why L7 kept the L6 settings. Since 57 % of the filtered failures
  follow fallback runs, whether retries change the result under the filter is open.
* **Interpretation, not tested in L7 (pb2-m2).** A possible explanation: the filter is as
  cautious as its model's uncertainty requires, the policies drive closer to the limit than
  the model can certify, and what the filter overrides is the policies' own robustness. That
  the policies handle car and lag errors through their observation history is a hypothesis
  suggested by L5 / L6; L7 does not test it.
* For H-C (Part C) this means: with the main filter, S3's "with the filter" laps will be fewer
  than without; S1 (without the filter) is what decides H-C.

### Reading

* **H-C, narrowed to the failure-start curriculum, is not supported.** The pre-registered
  comparison (median seeds, final checkpoints) does not support it; its result is significant
  in the opposite direction (the median F policy completes fewer test laps than the median P
  policy). At the level of seeds the groups do not differ significantly (descriptive, p = 0.69):
  F's distribution is wider and its median lower. Together with H-B (Part B: the safety
  filter lowers completions), neither L7 hypothesis holds; the pre-written conclusion for "H-C
  fails while H-B holds" does not apply, since H-B does not hold either.
* What is measured beyond the tests: the spread between seeds and between neighbouring
  checkpoints of one run is as large as any difference between the groups, and larger in F.
  **Interpretation, not tested in L7:** with SAC at these settings the final checkpoint is a
  noisy draw from an oscillating training process, and restarting 30 % of the episodes at
  recent failures may amplify the oscillation (the policy fixes one spot and loses another);
  L7 does not test this mechanism.
* The best test results of L7 come from validation-selected checkpoints (104 and 105 of 105

## Literature check (protocol section 1, before the report; 2026-10-17)

Web search and the arXiv abstract pages; abstracts read, not full papers, unless stated. The
search is not exhaustive.

* D. Kalaria, Q. Lin, J. M. Dolan, *Disturbance Observer-based Control Barrier Functions with
  Residual Model Learning for Safe Reinforcement Learning*, IROS 2025 (arXiv:2410.06570; the
  venue confirmed by the University of Houston's publication list, the proceedings page and
  DOI not found). A CBF safety filter on a nominal model with a disturbance observer and a
  learned residual, used **during training** (unsafe actions are filtered and penalised);
  Safety-gym Point / Car and an F1/10 car. The abstract does not report the policy without the
  filter. Relation to L7: this is the route of the dropped group M (a filter in training, A7);
  L7 filtered fixed policies only at deployment and tested a failure-start curriculum without a
  filter. L7's results neither confirm nor contradict theirs.
* B. Tearle, K. P. Wabersich, A. Carron, M. N. Zeilinger, *A predictive safety filter for
  learning-based racing control* (arXiv:2102.11907, 2021). An MPC safety filter keeping a
  miniature car on the track around manual driving and imitation-learned policies. The abstract
  reports that safety is kept in aggressive manoeuvres; it does not report completion with and
  without the filter or how often the filter intervenes. L7's main filter is of this family;
  what L7 adds is that measurement on unselected seeds, with false interventions – and its
  answer for this filter (lower completion, 88–99 % false interventions).
* D. D. Oh, D. P. Nguyen, H. Hu, J. F. Fisac, *Provably Optimal Reinforcement Learning under
  Safety Filtering*, IASEAI '26 (arXiv:2510.18082). A sufficiently permissive filter, kept
  in training and deployment, does not cost asymptotic performance; they recommend training
  and deploying with the most permissive filter available. L7's filter was not in training and
  far from permissive (Part B); L7 does not test their recommendation.
* R. Wu, R. Yang, D. D. Oh, D. Nguyen, H. Hu, *Turning Safety into Competence: Minimally
  Exploitable Robot Policies via Safety-Filtered Reinforcement Learning* (arXiv:2609.27312,
  2026): a policy trained with the filter it is deployed with. Same direction; not tested in
  L7.
* A. Zongo, P. Wei, *Runtime Safety Filtering for Learned Small UAS Separation Policies under
  GNSS Degradation*, DASC 2026 (arXiv:2607.10014): filtering the actions of a learned policy
  with discrete-time CBFs adds almost no safety; filtering its observations does. Another
  domain, a related observation: overriding a learned policy's actions did not add reliability.
* S. Strawn, N. Ayanian, L. Lindemann, *Conformal Predictive Safety Filter* (arXiv:2306.02551),
  and L. Lindemann, M. Cleaveland, G. Shim, G. J. Pappas, *Safe Planning in Dynamic
  Environments using Conformal Prediction*, IEEE RA-L 2023: conformal regions on the predicted
  motion of other agents. L7's estimate (c) uses split-conformal regions on the ego model's own
  errors; it failed L7's acceptance test (it over-covers: its bands are too wide).
* S. C. Y. Chan et al., *Measuring the Reliability of Reinforcement Learning Algorithms*, ICLR
  2020 (arXiv:1912.05663), and R. Agarwal et al., *Deep Reinforcement Learning at the Edge of the
  Statistical Precipice*, NeurIPS 2021 (arXiv:2108.13264): variability across runs and during
  training, and few-run comparisons that ignore it. Part C's seed spread and the jumps between
  neighbouring checkpoints are this known problem; L7's five seeds, unselected final checkpoints
  and median seeds follow from it, and S1's lap-level p value does not contain it (pc2-m1).
* Failure-targeted training: B. Eysenbach et al., *Leave no Trace: Learning to Reset for Safe
  and Autonomous Reinforcement Learning*, ICLR 2018 (arXiv:1711.06782) – learned resets and a
  curriculum that emerges from them, not restarts before failure positions. Found but not read:
  a genetic curriculum built from failure scenarios (Song et al.) and on-board RL for racing with
  a safety filter during training (RLC 2025; the PDF could not be read here).

Outcome: the works cited by the ROADMAP for L7 exist as cited. In this check, no work was found
that measures, for racing, whether a deployment-time predictive safety filter around policies
trained without it changes their completion rate on unselected seeds, with false interventions;
nor a pre-registered test of failure-position restarts. The recent safety-filter literature
points to training with a permissive filter – the route L7 could not take (A6, A7) and lists as
work after L7.
